# Intuition
The problem requires identifying employees who earn more than their direct managers. Since both employees and managers are stored in the same table, the natural idea is to perform a **self-join**. By matching each employee with their manager using the `ManagerId` field, we can then compare their salaries.

# Approach
We join the `Employee` table with itself. One alias (`e`) represents the employee, and the other alias (`m`) represents the manager. The join condition `e.ManagerId = m.Id` pairs each employee with their manager. After that, we filter rows where the employee’s salary is greater than the manager’s salary. Finally, we select the employee’s name.

# Complexity
- **Time complexity:**  
  Let \(n\) be the number of rows in the table. A naive join could take \(O(n^2)\). With proper indexing on `Id` and `ManagerId`, the join can be optimized to approximately \(O(n)\) lookups.

- **Space complexity:**  
  \(O(1)\), since we only output the names of employees and do not store intermediate results beyond what the database engine handles internally.

# Code
```mysql
SELECT e.name AS Employee
FROM Employee e
JOIN Employee m
  ON e.managerId = m.id
WHERE e.salary > m.salary;
